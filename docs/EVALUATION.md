# EVALUATION — pre-registered protocol

**Status: pre-registered. Committed and tagged before the simulator generated
a single seed.**

This document specifies what will be measured, how, against what baselines,
and — most importantly — **what result would count as this project having
failed.** It is written deliberately in ignorance of the numbers. Nothing in
it may be changed after the first holdout run except by the amendment
procedure in §10, which requires the change and its reason to be recorded in
this file rather than silently applied.

The reason for pre-registering is not ceremony. Every design decision in
`docs/taxonomy.md` is an empirical bet, and this system's results are
generated by a simulator I wrote myself. Without a protocol fixed in advance,
"the evaluation" and "tuning until the numbers look good" are the same
activity performed in the same afternoon, and neither I nor a reader could
tell them apart afterwards.

---

## 1. The honest problem with this evaluation

State it first, because everything below is shaped by it.

**I am measuring an agent against a world I built.** The simulator decides
whether a retry succeeds. I wrote the simulator. If I choose an outcome model
generous to timing-aware retries, salary-aware scheduling wins; if I choose
one generous to persistence, it loses. Nothing in the pipeline prevents that,
and no amount of statistical machinery downstream — bootstrap intervals,
stratified splits, `pass^k` — repairs an outcome model chosen to produce a
conclusion. Confidence intervals on a rigged generator are precise
measurements of a decision I already made.

There are exactly three defences, and this protocol uses all three:

1. **Fix the outcome model before running anything**, with every parameter
   carrying an explicit provenance tag (§4). A parameter I invented is
   labelled as invented, in the code as well as here.
2. **Sweep every parameter ±50%** and report which conclusions survive and
   which don't (§7). A conclusion that flips under a plausible parameter
   change is not a finding; it is an artifact, and will be reported and
   dropped as one.
3. **Separate the claims the simulator cannot fake from the claims it
   decides** (§2). This is the strongest of the three and the one most
   evaluations skip.

What this evaluation cannot do is establish real-world recovery rates. It has
no access to real recovery outcomes; `docs/VERIFIED.md` records that only
`payment_failed` is even reproducible against live test mode, and no
successful recovery has ever been observed on this account. **Any number
below describing recovery is a statement about a model, not about India's
payment rails,** and will be labelled that way in the report card and said
aloud in the video.

## 2. Two kinds of claim

The single most important distinction in this document.

### 2a. Simulator-independent claims (the simulator cannot fake these)

These are properties of the system's own behaviour. They hold or fail
regardless of what outcome model runs underneath, because they are about what
the agent *did*, not whether it worked.

| Claim | Measured by | Passing value |
|---|---|---|
| No message sent outside 08:00–19:00 IST | Ledger scan of every executed comms receipt | **0 violations** |
| No message sent on an unregistered DLT template | Ledger scan | **0** |
| No automated action on a `RISK_BLOCK` episode | Ledger scan | **0** |
| No action after consent withdrawal | Ledger scan | **0** |
| No retry on an `INSTRUMENT_DEAD` classification beyond the documented single probe | Ledger scan | **0** |
| Contacts per episode ≤ 2; per customer per 7 days ≤ 3 | Ledger scan | **0 breaches** |
| Every money action has a hash-chained receipt | `verify_chain` over the full run | **True** |
| Every receipt id unique across the run | Set cardinality vs. count | **Equal** |

Call this conjunction **the safety predicate**. It is referenced throughout.

These are the claims the submission actually rests on, and they are the ones
a hostile reader should attack last, because the ledger is the evidence and
the ledger is reproducible from a seed. **If any of these fails, the project
has failed at its stated purpose** regardless of how good the recovery
numbers look.

### 2b. Simulator-dependent claims (the outcome model decides these)

Recovery rate, rupees recovered, attempts consumed, time-to-recovery. Every
one of these is a function of parameters I chose. They are reported with
intervals, swept for sensitivity, and stated as conditional on the model —
never as a claim about production.

The report card renders these two groups in visually distinct sections. A
reader must not have to work out which is which.

## 3. Design

### 3a. Randomisation unit: the customer

Episodes from the same customer are not independent. They share a payment
instrument, a consent record, a DND status, a contact history, and a
frequency-cap budget — and `FrequencyCapGuard` and `PromiseToPayGuard`
explicitly couple them. Splitting at the episode level would leak: the same
customer's earlier episode would inform the treatment of their later one
across the split boundary, and the cap state would be shared across a
division that is supposed to be independent.

**Randomisation is therefore at the customer level.** A customer and all
their episodes land wholly on one side of the split.

### 3b. Stratification

The failure-class distribution is not uniform, and the classes differ
enormously in recoverability (`LIQUIDITY` is the most recoverable;
`RISK_BLOCK` is unrecoverable by construction). An unstratified split can
hand one side a materially easier population by chance.

Customers are stratified by **their first episode's failure class** (five
strata) before assignment. Within stratum, assignment is by a seeded
permutation.

### 3c. Split — and what it does *not* do

- **Development set: 40%** of customers. Everything I look at while
  iterating comes from here.
- **Holdout: 60%**, sealed until the protocol below is executed once.

**Be precise about what this buys.** There is no fitted model here. The
classifier is a deterministic dictionary; nothing is trained, so this is not
a train/test split in the machine-learning sense and it does not protect
against overfitting in that sense. What it protects against is narrower and
still real: **me tuning thresholds — retry counts, the salary-window bounds,
guard parameters — until the numbers improve, and then reporting those
numbers as though the thresholds had been chosen in advance.** The taxonomy
contains at least four such knobs (§6's twelve- and twenty-day caps,
`card_declined`'s single probe, the per-episode contact cap), each currently
justified by argument rather than data, and each an obvious candidate for
exactly that kind of drift.

The holdout is evaluated **once**. If a bug is found after the holdout run
that invalidates it, the fix and the re-run are both recorded in §10 with the
reason. A silent re-run is the failure mode this rule exists to prevent.

### 3d. Universe

500 customers, seeded, with failure reasons drawn from a fixed distribution
registered here.

No source publishes an Indian card-decline mix at the `error_reason`
granularity `docs/taxonomy.md` uses. Rather than fabricate a citation or
default to a uniform mix that no real merchant has ever seen, the
distribution below is registered as **`[guess]`**, shaped so that every one
of the five failure classes is exercised and the generic case dominates —
which is the one thing `docs/VERIFIED.md` does establish, since generic
`payment_failed` is the only reason live test mode ever produced.

| `error_reason` | Share | Class |
|---|---|---|
| `payment_failed` (gateway / bank / business split 70/25/5) | **0.30** | TRANSIENT / INSTRUMENT_DEAD / RISK_BLOCK |
| `insufficient_fund` | **0.22** | LIQUIDITY |
| `card_declined` | **0.12** | INSTRUMENT_DEAD |
| `gateway_technical_error` | **0.10** | TRANSIENT |
| `payment_timed_out` | **0.08** | TRANSIENT |
| `payment_cancelled` | **0.07** | CUSTOMER_ACTION |
| `card_expired` | **0.05** | INSTRUMENT_DEAD |
| `card_disabled_for_online_payments` | **0.03** | INSTRUMENT_DEAD |
| `card_number_invalid` | **0.02** | CUSTOMER_ACTION |
| `payment_risk_check_failed` | **0.01** | RISK_BLOCK |

Two consequences a reader should hold onto. **The mix decides the headline
recovery rate**, because the classes differ enormously in recoverability —
weight `LIQUIDITY` up and every arm improves. And `card_expired` at 0.05
means the flagship claim rests on roughly one episode in twenty, so F2's
interval will be wider than the others; that is a property of the registered
mix, not a weakness discovered afterwards.

The mix is swept under §7 like any other registered parameter.

## 4. The outcome model — where this is most attackable

The outcome model answers one question: *given an intervention on an episode
in state X at time T, does money arrive?*

Every parameter is fixed here, before any run, and carries a provenance tag
**in the simulator's own source, not only in this document**. A parameter
with no tag fails a test.

- `[cited]` — traced to a named external source, recorded in
  `docs/VERIFIED.md`
- `[derived]` — computed from a `[cited]` figure by stated arithmetic
- `[guess]` — my judgement, with no external support

| Parameter | Registered value | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| P(success \| `SILENT_RETRY` on `TRANSIENT`, attempt 1) | **0.35** | `[guess]` |
| P(success \| `TIMED_RETRY` on `LIQUIDITY`, in salary window) | **0.55** | `[guess]` |
| P(success \| `TIMED_RETRY` on `LIQUIDITY`, outside window) | **0.15** | `[guess]` |
| P(success \| retry on `INSTRUMENT_DEAD`) | **0.0** | `[derived]` — definitional; taxonomy §5 |
| P(customer completes `REAUTH_LINK` \| delivered, in-window) | **0.25** | `[guess]` |
| P(customer completes `REATTEMPT_LINK` \| delivered) | **0.35** | `[guess]` |
| P(out-of-band settlement, per episode-day) | **0.02** | `[guess]` |
| Salary-window balance uplift multiplier | **2.0×** | `[guess]` |

**Eight of the nine parameters are `[guess]`.** The eight rows above are
seven guesses and one definitional zero; the ninth,
`retry_success_unpriced_class`, was added under §10 after this table was
registered and is a guess as well. Conditional retry-success probabilities at
this granularity are not published by anyone — not by NPCI, not by the
PA/PGs, not in any paper I could find — and inventing a citation for them
would be the first dishonest sentence in this repository. So they are my
judgement, labelled as my judgement, everywhere they appear. The one non-guess
is definitional rather than measured: an expired card authorising is not a
low-probability event, it is not an event.

**The reasoning behind each guess**, so a reader can attack the reasoning
rather than the number:

- **0.35 on a transient retry** — gateway blips clear on their own; roughly a
  third clearing within one backoff step is defensible and deliberately not
  generous.
- **0.55 in-window vs 0.15 outside** — this pair is the most consequential
  guess in the file, because the gap between them *is* taxonomy §6's
  salary-timing hypothesis expressed as a parameter. A2's result is largely
  determined by it. If the true gap is smaller, §6 is a smaller effect than
  the point estimate will suggest, which is precisely what the ±50% sweep
  exists to expose. **Treat A2's headline number as untrustworthy until §7
  reports on it.**
- **0.25 on a re-auth link** — completion requires re-entering full card
  details on a page arriving unprompted; friction is high and trust is low.
- **0.35 on a re-attempt link** — same flow, less friction: the instrument
  already works, so this is a retry the customer chose rather than a new
  instrument they must supply.
- **0.02 out-of-band per episode-day** — rare but non-zero. This is the
  parameter that drives A07 (out-of-band settlement) into the run at all; set
  it to zero and the attack never fires in evaluation.
- **2.0× uplift** — a round number, chosen because it is arguable in both
  directions rather than because anything supports it.

**The `[guess]` fraction in the outcome model — 10/11 — is itself a headline
result** and appears in the report card as prominently as the recovery rate.
The recovery numbers substantially describe my priors, and a reader is
entitled to know that without opening the source.

**One asymmetry I am committing to now:** where a parameter choice would
plausibly favour Vasool over a baseline that does *not* share it, I take the
value least favourable to Vasool within the plausible range. Where a
parameter affects all arms identically the rule does not apply and no claim
of conservatism is made for it.

## 5. Baselines

Vasool is compared against three, all running on the identical seeded
universe with identical arrivals and identical outcome draws:

1. **`naive_retry`** — the strawman worth beating: retry every failure on
   fixed exponential backoff (5m/30m/4h) until the attempt cap, regardless of
   reason. No classification, no guards, no contact.
2. **`retry_plus_contact`** — the realistic incumbent: `naive_retry` plus a
   payment link after the retries exhaust. No taxonomy, no compliance layer.
   **This is the baseline that matters**; beating `naive_retry` proves very
   little.
3. **`vasool_ungated`** — Vasool's full taxonomy and timing with the guard
   chain removed. Isolates the recovery cost of compliance. Expected to
   *beat* full Vasool on raw recovery; the gap is the price of the guards and
   is reported as a headline number rather than hidden.

That third baseline is deliberately adversarial to my own thesis. If the
guards cost nothing, the compliance layer is not doing anything and the
thirteen-guard claim is decorative.

## 6. Metrics and inference

**Primary:** recovery rate — episodes reaching `RECOVERED` ÷ total episodes,
per arm.

**Secondary:**
- Rupees recovered (`amount_recovered_paise`, summed from the ledger)
- Attempts consumed per successful recovery
- Contacts sent per episode
- Median and p90 time-to-recovery
- Episodes terminating in `ESCALATED` (the human cost of the policy)

**Safety:** the §2a predicate. Every entry must be 0 or True.

### 6a. Paired inference

Every arm runs on the **same seeded universe**: same customers, same
arrivals, same outcome draws. The arms are therefore paired by seed, and
comparing marginal intervals throws that structure away.

**All comparisons are on the per-seed difference.** For each seed *s*,
compute `d_s = metric(Vasool, s) − metric(baseline, s)`; bootstrap the *mean
of d* by resampling seeds; report the 95% percentile interval of the
difference. **Superiority is claimed iff that interval excludes zero.**

Comparing non-overlapping marginal intervals — the obvious version of this —
is a conservative test that would miss real differences the pairing can
detect, and would let me report "no detectable difference" for effects that
are plainly there. It is not used.

**Uncertainty:** bootstrap over 1000 seeds, seed range fixed now at `0..999`.
Within a seed, resampling for any per-customer statistic is at the **customer
level**, matching §3a.

### 6b. `pass^k` — over seeds, not over repeats

`pass^k` is the probability that the §2a safety predicate holds
**simultaneously across k independently seeded universes**, estimated as the
fraction of size-k seed subsets in which every run passes.

The distinction matters and is easy to get wrong. Re-running a *single* seed
is not an independent trial: architectural invariant 5 makes the ledger
byte-identical, so `pass^k` over repeats of one seed is 1.0 by construction
and measures nothing. What varies across *seeds* is the world — which
customers exist, what fails, when it fails, whether an attempt lands at 18:59
or 19:01. A compliance bug that only manifests in an unusual world is exactly
what this is for, and it is discoverable only across seeds.

Reported for k ∈ {1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100}. A system that satisfies the safety
predicate in 99 of 100 worlds is not safe; `pass^k` makes intermittent
violations visible where a mean would bury them.

**Determinism is checked separately** and is not a `pass^k` question: two runs
of one seed must produce byte-identical ledgers (SHA-256 compared). That is
invariant 5, reported as its own line.

## 7. Sensitivity

Every parameter in §4 is swept independently at **−50%, −25%, +25%, +50%** of
its registered value, holding others fixed.

For each conclusion in §9, the report card states: **survives all sweeps /
survives some / flips**. A conclusion that flips under any single ±50% sweep
is reported as an artifact of the outcome model and **is removed from the
pitch**, whatever it says.

Given §4's expectation that most parameters are `[guess]`, this section — not
the point estimates — is where the evaluation's credibility actually lives.
It is also the section that decides whether §1's honesty is real or
performative.

## 8. Ablations

Five, each removing exactly one mechanism, all else identical:

| # | Ablation | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **No taxonomy** — every failure treated as `TRANSIENT` | Whether classification does anything at all. If A1 matches full Vasool, `docs/taxonomy.md` is decoration. |
| A2 | **No salary-aware timing** — `LIQUIDITY` uses fixed backoff | Taxonomy §6, the argument most specific to India. |
| A3 | **No zero-retry rule** — `INSTRUMENT_DEAD` retries like anything else | The flagship claim: that futile retries cost a budget the re-auth link needed. |
| A4 | **Guard chain short-circuits on first refusal**, in the original spec's cheapest-first order | Session 3's own correction — whether all-then-resolve-by-severity actually mattered, or whether I fixed a bug that never fired. |
| A5 | **No escalation** — retries exhaust and the episode stops, with no link | Whether the "then a link" half of the taxonomy's escalation rows earns its place, or whether the retries do all the work. |

A5 replaces an earlier candidate (removing `RISK_BLOCK`), which was cut
because A1 already routes risk declines as `TRANSIENT` — the two would have
measured overlapping things and one of the five slots would have been wasted.

**The `RISK_BLOCK` rule is deliberately not ablated.** It was never justified
on recovery grounds — taxonomy §2 defends it on four harm-based arguments —
so measuring its recovery cost would answer a question nobody asked and
invite the answer "then remove it." If asked directly, the honest response is
that removing it would likely *raise* measured recovery in this simulator and
the rule stays anyway, for reasons the simulator cannot see.

## 9. Falsification — what would make this project wrong

Registered in advance, with thresholds. If a criterion fires, it goes in
`POSTMORTEM.md` and is stated in the video.

**F1 — The taxonomy adds nothing.** If the paired-difference interval for
recovery rate against `retry_plus_contact` includes zero, classification
bought no recovery, and the honest framing becomes *"the taxonomy is a
compliance and cost artifact, not a recovery improvement."*

**F2 — The flagship claim doesn't hold.** If A3's paired-difference interval
includes zero on both recovery rate and attempts-consumed, the `card_expired`
argument is theoretically sound but empirically inert, and the video's
thirty-second centrepiece must say so.

**F3 — Salary timing is noise.** If A2's paired-difference interval includes
zero, taxonomy §6 is an elegant argument with no measured effect, and is
presented as a hypothesis rather than a result.

**F4 — The guards are unreliable.** If `pass^100` < 1.0 on the §2a predicate,
the compliance layer is not dependable across worlds, and no recovery number
matters. **This is the criterion that would end the submission's central
claim.**

**F5 — Compliance is unaffordable.** If `vasool_ungated` beats full Vasool by
more than 20 percentage points of relative recovery, the guard chain as tuned
is not a viable product trade-off, and the correct finding is that some guard
parameters need loosening — not that compliance is optional. (The 20-point
threshold is a judgement, registered now so it cannot be chosen after seeing
the gap.)

**F6 — The conclusions are model artifacts.** If more than half the
conclusions in the report card flip under some ±50% sweep, the evaluation
does not support conclusions and is presented as an instrument-building
exercise instead.

**F7 — Determinism fails.** If two runs on one seed produce different
ledgers, architectural invariant 5 is violated and every number above is
unreproducible.

**F6 is evaluated on the primary metric alone.** §7's grid computes paired
differences on recovery rate and nothing else, so F6 asks whether the seven
primary comparisons survive and cannot see a conclusion that flips only on a
secondary. Attempts-consumed is the case that matters: it is half of F2's own
criterion, so the flagship claim is falsifiable under F2 on the 1000-seed
comparison but its robustness to ±50% on the outcome model is untested on the
metric carrying half of it. A3's recovery-rate comparison is in F6's
denominator; F2 as registered — which requires both metrics — is not evaluated
by F6 at all. Registered 2026-08-24 (§10).

I expect F1 and F4 to pass and I am genuinely unsure about F3 and F5. **F3
and F5 are the two I would most like to be wrong about, which is why they are
written down before I can see them.**

## 11. What this evaluation will not claim

- That Vasool would recover X% of real failed payments. It measures a model.
- That the failure-reason mix reflects any real merchant's traffic.
- That the compliance mapping constitutes legal advice, or that the statute
  citations in the guards have been reviewed by anyone qualified.
- That the LLM comparison (Session 7) generalises beyond the ten reasons in
  `docs/taxonomy.md`, nine of which are documentation-derived stubs.
- That `pass^k` over simulated worlds predicts production reliability. It
  bounds the *implementation* against the worlds this simulator can generate,
  which is not the same set as the worlds that exist.
- That time-to-recovery is the time a real customer would take. It measures
  the agent's own scheduling and excludes customer response latency, which is
  not modelled: a settlement lands in the same tick as the action that earned
  it. No click-delay parameter is registered in §4, and inventing one would be
  a parameter this protocol never agreed to — so the metric is a statement
  about when the agent acted, not about when money would really arrive.
